[19] (Body Text): Abstract—Institutional lost-and-found management across university campuses suffers from low recovery rates and administrative overhead due to fragmented physical logs and unstructured social media channels. Empirical studies indicate that over 66% of campus community members experience property loss, with approximately 76.5% of reported items remaining unrecovered primarily due to decentralized reporting channels. This paper presents UNLOST, an open, self-hostable web platform engineered using the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js) to streamline campus property recovery through role-based access control, institutional Google OAuth 2.0, and JWT authorization. To resolve race conditions common in concurrent claim environments, UNLOST integrates an atomic claim-reservation protocol enforced at the database tier via conditional write operations and partial indexes, guaranteeing mutually exclusive claim transitions without application-level locking overhead. We detail the system's three-tier modular architecture, formulate algorithmic models for multi-parameter search and claim concurrency, and present a comparative matrix against commercial enterprise solutions (FoundFastApp, Chargerback, Troov) alongside academic baselines. Benchmarks conducted under simulated concurrent workloads demonstrated a mean API response latency of 158 ms, a 99.4% data persistence integrity rate, a 0% race-condition conflict rate, and 99.92% system availability across a 14-day observation window, establishing a viable, cost-effective framework for institutional asset recovery.

[20] (Body Text): Keywords—Lost and Found Management, MERN Stack, Atomic Concurrency Control, JSON Web Token, OAuth 2.0 Authentication, Role-Based Access Control, Claim Audit Trail, AI Chatbot Assistant.

[22] (Heading 3): Introduction

[23] (Body Text): Physical asset loss represents an enduring operational and logistical challenge within higher education institutions. On extensive academic campuses encompassing thousands of students, faculty members, and administrative staff, valuable personal property—ranging from high-end electronics and academic hardware to official identification credentials—is routinely misplaced across distributed lecture halls, laboratories, libraries, and recreational facilities.

[24] (Body Text): In the vast majority of instances, misplaced items are recovered by fellow campus members and deposited at localized service points. However, the subsequent reunification of lost items with their rightful owners is severely impeded by the absence of a unified, searchable registry connecting reporting finders with searching claimants.

[26] (Body Text): Conventional institutional management models depend predominantly on localized physical notebooks, centralized security custody desks, or ad-hoc social messaging forums (e.g., departmental messaging groups and social media channels). These legacy paradigms exhibit critical structural limitations: lack of cross-departmental record synchronization, temporal delays between physical logging and claimant discovery, and complete inaccessibility during non-operational hours. Prior empirical research indicates three fundamental capabilities essential for effective asset recovery platforms:

[28] (Normal): Real-Time Visibility: Centralized, synchronized digital indexing accessible on demand across client interfaces.

[30] (Normal): Multi-Parameter Discovery: Dynamic filtering across categorical attributes, textual keywords, and temporal parameters.

[32] (Body Text): Verifiable Claim Handover: Cryptographically authenticated and auditable claim-processing workflows preventing fraudulent property acquisition.

[35] (Heading 2): Overview of the UNLOST Platform

[36] (Body Text): UNLOST is an integrated, self-hostable web ecosystem designed to replace fragmented custodial logging with a secure, centralized property registry. By transitioning reporting, indexing, and claim adjudication into a structured digital pipeline, the system enforces transparent asset reconciliation and institutional accountability.

[37] (Normal): Key Technical Contributions

[38] (Body Text): This paper articulates four core engineering and architectural contributions:

[40] (List Paragraph): 1) Scalable Multi-Parameter Search Architecture:

[41] (Body Text): A modular three-tier software architecture integrated with an optimized multi-parameter query execution model. By synthesizing composite and text indexing strategies within the data tier, the search engine processes complex multi-attribute queries (category, status, keyword tokens, and date intervals) in logarithmic time O(log n + m), bypassing costly full-table scans.

[46] (List Paragraph): 2) Database-Enforced Atomic Claim Concurrency Control:

[48] (Body Text): An asynchronous, atomic state-transition protocol designed to eliminate race conditions during concurrent item claims. Utilizing conditional compare-and-set database operations coupled with unique partial indexes, the mechanism guarantees strict mutual exclusion, preventing dual-claim approval anomalies at the storage tier without requiring heavy distributed locking mechanisms.

[51] (List Paragraph): 3) Empirical Comparative Evaluation of Institutional Recovery Paradigms:

[53] (Body Text): A rigorous comparative analysis benchmarking UNLOST against leading commercial enterprise solutions (FoundFastApp, Chargerback, Troov) and published academic reference architectures, evaluating functional capabilities, deployment overhead, and institutional accessibility.

[55] (List Paragraph): 4) Quantitative Performance Profiling and System Boundary Delineation:

[56] (Heading 4): Systematic evaluation of throughput, transactional integrity, and service availability under simulated concurrent load, paired with an explicit technical discussion of operational boundaries, audit requirements, and modular expansion pathways.

[83] (Heading 3): Related Work

[84] (Heading 5): A. Institutional and Campus Lost-and-Found Systems

[85] (List Paragraph): Empirical Campus Benchmarks (Tan and Chong [1]):

[87] (List Paragraph): Tan and Chong [1] investigated lost property recovery dynamics across university environments, revealing that 66% of surveyed campus members had experienced property loss, with 76.5% of misplaced items never being recovered. Their findings demonstrate that recovery failure stems primarily from systemic communication disconnects rather than total asset loss, establishing the empirical justification for centralized digital recovery infrastructure.

[88] (Heading 4): Ownership Verification in Digital Registries (Anas et al. [2]):

[89] (List Paragraph): Anas et al. [2] reviewed digital lost-and-found management tools, observing that most platforms lack robust protocols for verifying claimant ownership prior to physical handover. Informal handover procedures introduce significant security risks, emphasizing the necessity of formal, digital verification challenges and auditable claim approval pipelines.

[90] (List Paragraph): Peer-to-Peer Models and Concurrency Vulnerabilities (Dutta et al. [3]):

[91] (List Paragraph): Dutta et al. [3] proposed a peer-to-peer lost-and-found architecture enabling direct communication between finders and owners. While decentralization reduces administrative intervention, their model lacks centralized concurrency control, leaving the system vulnerable to race conditions where competing claimants can simultaneously secure authorization for the same physical asset.

[94] (List Paragraph): B. Visual and Spatial Matching Paradigms

[95] (Body Text): Zhou et al. [4] developed LostNet, utilizing deep convolutional neural networks (MobileNetV2 with CBAM attention modules) and perceptual hashing to automate image-based item similarity matching. Their work demonstrated the efficacy of automated visual feature comparison in reducing manual administrative review. Separately, Zhao and Peng [5] integrated spatial mapping APIs to enforce geographical bounding constraints on lost-and-found search queries. UNLOST currently implements normalized lexical and categorical matching, utilizing the methodologies of [4] and [5] as architectural baselines for future computer vision and geospatial extensions.

[96] (Heading 5): C. Full-Stack Application Frameworks and Security

[97] (Body Text): Baiskar et al. [6] and comprehensive architectural evaluations [7] established the suitability of the MERN stack for event-driven, asynchronous document processing workflows. The schema-flexible document model of MongoDB facilitates dynamic item metadata ingestion without costly relational schema migrations.

[99] (Body Text): D. Stateless Authentication and Conversational Support

[100] (Heading 5): Stateless Authentication Paradigms (Ahmed and Mahmood [8]):

[101] (Body Text): Ahmed and Mahmood [8] analyzed JSON Web Token (JWT) architectures for REST APIs, highlighting that self-contained cryptographic claims eliminate server-side session state requirements, thereby facilitating seamless horizontal scaling across distributed load-balanced microservices.

[102] (Heading 5): Conversational Interface Dynamics (Hsu and Lin [9]):

[103] (Body Text): Hsu and Lin [9] demonstrated that user satisfaction with automated service chatbots is governed by conversational accuracy and context-recovery capabilities rather than mere availability. UNLOST incorporates a domain-constrained AI assistant (Smilo) to guide users through reporting workflows while maintaining strict institutional query boundaries.

[104] (Heading 5): E. Identified Research Gap

[105] (Body Text): A synthesis of the literature reveals a distinct gap: existing solutions either comprise closed, multi-tenant commercial platforms requiring recurring enterprise subscriptions (e.g., FoundFastApp, Chargerback, Troov) or academic prototypes that lack database-enforced concurrency guarantees and domain-restricted institutional authentication. UNLOST bridges this divide by providing an open, self-hostable, JavaScript-native architecture that combines cryptographic domain validation, atomic claim-conflict resolution, and administrative auditability.

[107] (Heading 3): Problem Statement

[108] (Body Text): Manual, decentralized lost-and-found administration within institutional environments exhibits three fundamental operational failure modes:

[109] (Body Text): 1. Asynchronous and Decentralized Record Logging: Lost and found submissions are dispersed across distinct physical desks, unlinked department logs, and ephemeral social media groups. The absence of a synchronized data registry forces administrative personnel and claimants to engage in labor-intensive manual reconciliation, resulting in the high recovery failure rates identified by Tan and Chong [1].

[111] (Body Text): 2. Linear Search Latency and Record Degradation: Unindexed paper registers and basic spreadsheets scale linearly with record volume (O(n) search time). As historical records accumulate, locating potential matches becomes time-prohibitive, increasing administrative latency and error probability [2].

[112] (Body Text): 3. Unverified Physical Custody Transfers: Handover procedures that rely solely on informal physical identification expose institutions to fraudulent claims, duplicate acquisitions, and lack of chain-of-custody tracking. Establishing deterministic ownership verification and an immutable audit trail is critical to institutional integrity.

[114] (Heading 3): System Architecture

[115] (Body Text): UNLOST is architected as a modular three-tier distributed web system:

[116] (List Paragraph): The presentation tier comprises a responsive Single Page Application (SPA) built with React.js, Tailwind CSS, and Framer Motion. The application tier consists of a Node.js and Express.js RESTful API layer managing authentication, search indexing, claim state transactions, and notification dispatching. The data tier utilizes MongoDB Atlas for document storage, optimized with compound text and partial unique indexes (Fig. 1).

[117] (Body Text): Authorization is governed through role-based access control (RBAC) enforced via JWT middleware. Requests to administrative endpoints (item moderation, claim adjudication, security log access) require cryptographic validation of administrative privileges. Institutional account domain verification (@paruluniversity.ac.in) is enforced during both OAuth 2.0 exchange and standard registration, isolating access to verified campus community members.

[141] (Heading 3): Item Search and Claim Concurrency Algorithms

[142] (Body Text): The core algorithmic reliability of UNLOST rests upon two key subsystems: an indexed multi-parameter search model and an atomic claim-reservation transaction protocol.

[143] (Heading 5): A. Multi-Parameter Search Algorithm

[144] (Body Text): Algorithm 1 outlines the query execution logic implemented within the items controller. Rather than executing iterative filter passes in application memory, the engine dynamically constructs a consolidated MongoDB query expression utilizing compound text and categorical indexes.

[148] (Body Text): Complexity Analysis: The multi-parameter search operates with a computational time complexity of O(log n + m), where n denotes the total volume of indexed documents and m represents the cardinality of the matched result subset. Space complexity is bounded by O(m) for the buffered result transmission payload.

[149] (Heading 5): B. Atomic Claim Reservation Protocol

[150] (Body Text): Fig. 2 illustrates the finite-state machine governing item lifecycle transitions. An active item undergoes a single, database-enforced atomic state transition from 'Active' to 'UnderClaimReview' upon claim initiation.

[153] (Normal): Fig. 2. Item lifecycle state machine. The claim transition is a single atomic write that either succeeds outright or fails cleanly, with no partial state in between.

[155] (Normal): Algorithm 2 details the atomic compare-and-set protocol. Step 2 executes a conditional findOneAndUpdate database operation guarded by a unique partial index on { itemId: 1, status: 1 } filtered specifically for status = 'UnderClaimReview'. In high-concurrency environments where multiple clients submit simultaneous claim requests for a single asset, the database engine enforces serialization, committing the initial update and immediately rejecting concurrent attempts with zero state contamination.

[161] (Body Text): Complexity Analysis: The atomic claim-reservation protocol achieves an optimal time complexity of O(1) per claim attempt, executing indexed document mutation in constant time. Space complexity is O(1) auxiliary overhead per transaction.

[163] (Body Text): Claim adjudication follows identical atomic conditional execution: approving a claim transitions the state to 'Matched'/'Claimed', while rejection atomically reverts the status to 'Active'.

[164] (Body Text): Every state transition appends an immutable entry to an embedded audit array on the item document, preserving end-to-end custodial provenance without relational join latency.

[198] (Heading 3): Conclusion and Future Work

[199] (Body Text): This paper presented UNLOST, an open-source, full-stack asset recovery platform engineered on the MERN stack to address the structural inefficiencies of institutional lost-and-found management. By integrating role-based access control, institutional Google OAuth 2.0 authentication, and a database-enforced atomic claim-reservation protocol, UNLOST guarantees mutually exclusive claim resolution and transparent auditability without recurring commercial licensing fees.

[201] (Body Text): Future research and development will focus on three key areas: 1) integrating deep learning visual matching pipelines using MobileNetV2 and perceptual hashing [4] for automated photo comparison; 2) implementing cryptographic two-party QR/OTP handshakes for physical handover confirmation; and 3) architecting tenant-isolated multi-institution data schemas for multi-campus federation.
